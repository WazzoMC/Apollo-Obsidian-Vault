To actually get good at something, you have to repeatedly do *it.*

I know it sounds trivial, stupid almost, but it is entirely true and something we cannot ommit in our lives. You cannot expect to be able to do something by doing anything else other than *it.* Let me explain:

If you want to write an amazing article about the AI Liability Gap, for example, you cannot expect to do that by doing tons of research, or curating a well-informed source bank. Sure, that helps. Matter of fact, I don't think you can even write such an article without research. 

The problem is getting stuck at that phase, and not advancing onto practice fast enough. We humans seem to love postponing hard things until the last minute. And sometimes, we do that by covering up our procrastination with what we think are "productive" activities.

Research may be productive for the first day or two, but, after that, you need to start writing your article. At some point, you *will* have to start writing that first draft. And you know what?
All of that is done by *writing.*

If you want to get faster when *karting,* then you have to *join more and more races.*

Even for school tests and exams, scientists found that the best way to get a good grade when taking a test at school is by **repeatedly testing yourself at home.** Funny, right?

So, keep this in mind next time you try to achieve anything. Preparation and theory are amazing, and we definitely shouldn't ignore them, but, offering more time to theory rather than practice risks undermining your true potential. Research stops being productive when additional research has lower value than beginning the task.

To actually get good at something, you have to repeatedly do *it.*

Practice makes perfect.
