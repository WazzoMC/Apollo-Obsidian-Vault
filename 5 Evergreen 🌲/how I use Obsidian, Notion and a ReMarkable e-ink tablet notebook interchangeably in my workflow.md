#### Obsidian:
Obsidian is mainly used as my second-brain, a place where I can hold or capture information I want to use later in life, but don't necessarily want to spend time and energy trying to remember it.

Wether those ideas or pieces of information are intrinsic or external (my own thoughts or just something I want to capture), that information gets saved into a Project / Area folder. Just like in the [[PARA Method]]. 

P.S: I also have Obsidian on my phone so I can look through my notes / quickly capture thoughts even when I'm not at my laptop. This way of using the "Second Brain" is heavily inspired by the video "[[Second Brain Organisation by Ali Abdaal]] ." He mainly uses apple notes, but [[I don't like closed ecosystems]].

#### Notion:
Notion is used in a different way - at least in my workflow. 
Its UI and features, I would argue, provide a more user-friendly approach. The AI integrations (such as its built-in paid one & the ChatGPT Plugin,) Page Embeddings as sub-pages, and the many Templates available on the marketplace make it superior for carrying out your Projects when compared to Obsidian.

That's just my opinion though. And again, what I mainly use it for is Projects.

Writing a Youtube Video Script? Notion. 
Planning out the cinematic shots while embedding your ReMarkable drawings? Notion!
Writing a 126 page essay with lore for your Dungeons & Dragons campaign about a horrific murder mystery of a magical cookie man where the player-party has to go and rescue the rest of the cookie kingdom from a dangerous 3000 year old witch? I mean- yeah... you *could* use Notion, you Murder Mystery documentary addict.  

The idea is simple: info you want to remember/use for later goes into Obsidian (your second brain), and your actual use of that info goes into Notion towards your projects.

#### ReMarkable:
The ReMarkable is a really interesting tool here because it gives us the ability to think without distractions. The 2 apps we just listed beforehand are both digital. That means opening a digital tool to use. That means being exposed to the many distractions the digital world possesses.

Not only that, but the ability to simply *draw* and write in any way you want, completely free-form, is in my opinion completely superior to simply typing for what I use my e-ink tablet for.

So... what do I use it for, you may ask?

I use it for thinking. 

While Obsidian is used to capture information (intrinsic or external,) my physical or digital notebook is used to think. Any type of brainstorming, planning, or journaling belongs in there. A digital knowledge garden is great for *saving* things, but I strongly believe [[the best place to think is on pen and paper]].

I also use it another way, with the Cornell Note-Taking method when learning.
I first [[survey and question the learning material before reading]] it.
That, along with with The 5R's of Note-Taking by Ali Abdaal.
